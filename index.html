BLOCK A
Question focus: how much sleep teenagers need, and why it is important.
Step 1: Select
  • Experts recommend 8–10 hours a night.
  • Many teenagers get far less.
  • The brain stores what it has learned, so a rested student remembers more.
  • Too little sleep causes irritability, anxiety, low mood and poor concentration.
  • Sleep helps the body grow and repair muscles.
  • Sleep helps fight illness.

Left out: the opening about parents (introduction); “not laziness” (comment); cramming harming (repeats point 3); the cousin (aside); athletes (example); the last sentence (comment).
Step 2: Combine
  • Points 1 + 2 are joined with “yet” (contrast).
  • Point 3 uses “so” (result).
  • Points 5 + 6 become one list.

Step 3: Own words
  • “sorts and stores” → “file away”
  • “irritable, anxious or low” → “bad-tempered, worried”
  • “fight off illness” → “resistance to disease”

Step 4: Summary (1/3rd)
Teenagers are advised to get eight to ten hours’ rest nightly, yet many manage far less. Rest allows the brain to file away new knowledge, so a well-rested learner recalls more than someone who studies through the night. Lack of sleep can make young people bad-tempered, worried or unable to focus. Sleep further aids physical growth, muscle repair and disease resistance.
BLOCK B
Focus: How plastic reaches the oceans, and the harm it causes.
Step 1: Select
  • It comes from beaches, rivers and rubbish heaps.
  • It lasts hundreds of years.
  • Animals swallow it as food and may starve.
  • Animals get tangled, injured or drowned.
  • It breaks into microplastics, which fish eat.
  • It passes up the food chain into human seafood.
  • Polluted beaches drive away tourists.
  • Clean-ups are expensive.

Left out: “millions of tonnes” (general introduction); the turtles, seabirds, seals and dolphins (examples of points 3–4); “smaller than a grain of rice” (description); the photograph of the turtle (aside); the final sentence (opinion).
Step 2: Combine
  • Points 1 + 2 are joined with “where”.
  • Points 3 + 4 are joined with “while” (two kinds of harm).
  • Points 5 + 6 become one sentence.
  • Points 7 + 8 are joined with “and”.

Step 3: Own words
  • “rubbish heaps” → “dumps”
  • “hundreds of years” → “centuries”
  • “drive away tourists” → “deter tourists”

Step 4: Summary
Plastic enters the sea from beaches, rivers and dumps, where it may survive for centuries. Sea creatures swallow it, confusing it with food, and can starve, while others get caught in it and are hurt or drowned. Broken into microplastics, it is consumed by tiny fish and travels through the food chain, even into seafood for people. Littered beaches also deter tourists and are costly to clear.
BLOCK C
Focus: the reasons street food is popular.
Step 1: Select
  • It is cheaper than restaurants.
  • People on small budgets can afford it.
  • It is quick to serve.
  • It is varied: every region and street is different.
  • It is social: people gather and share city life.

Left out: the smells in the opening (description); pav bhaji and peanuts (examples); the writer’s pocket money (aside); “point, pay and eat” (description); family-run stalls and the “bank guards its gold” comparison (aside); the chaat and kulfi examples.
Step 2: Combine
  • Points 1 + 2 are joined with “so” (result).
  • Point 4 uses “because” (the passage gives the reason).

Step 3: Own words
  • “people on tight budgets” → “those with limited money”
  • “quick” → “convenient… within minutes”
  • “every street” → “every lane”

Step 4: Summary
Street food is cheap compared with restaurant meals, so those with limited money can enjoy it. It is also convenient, as vendors prepare and serve snacks within minutes. Its wide range attracts people because every region, and even every lane, has its own dishes. Lastly, it brings people together, giving friends, relatives and strangers a place to gather and enjoy city life.

BLOCK A
Ask any parent of a teenager, and they will tell you the same thing: teenagers seem to sleep at the strangest hours. Yet scientists say that sleep is not laziness but one of the most important things a growing body can do. Most experts recommend that teenagers sleep between eight and ten hours every night, although surveys suggest that many get far less, especially on school nights.
During sleep, the brain sorts and stores what it has learned during the day. A student who revises for a test and then sleeps well is likely to remember more than one who stays up all night revising. In other words, the late-night cramming session that so many students swear by often does more harm than good.
Sleep also affects mood. Teenagers who regularly sleep too little are more likely to feel irritable, anxious or low, and they often find it harder to concentrate in class. My own cousin, who once tried to survive an entire exam week on four hours a night, says he spent most of it snapping at everyone and staring blankly at his textbooks.
Finally, sleep matters for the body. It is during deep sleep that the body releases hormones that help it grow and repair muscles, which is why athletes take rest so seriously. A good night’s sleep also helps the body fight off illness. Sleep, it turns out, is not time wasted; it is time the body and brain put to very good use.

Summarise what the text says about how much sleep teenagers need and why sleep is important.

BLOCK B
Every year, millions of tonnes of plastic find their way into the world’s oceans. Some of it is dropped on beaches, some is washed down rivers, and some simply blows off rubbish heaps on windy days. Once it reaches the sea, it can float for decades, because most plastic takes hundreds of years to break down.
The most visible victims are sea animals. Turtles often mistake floating plastic bags for jellyfish, their favourite food, and seabirds feed bottle caps and lighters to their chicks. Animals that swallow plastic may feel full while actually starving. Others, such as seals and dolphins, become tangled in old fishing nets and plastic rings, which can injure or drown them. Anyone who has seen photographs of a turtle trapped in a net will not easily forget them.
Plastic does not disappear even when it breaks apart. Sunlight and waves slowly crack it into tiny pieces called microplastics, many smaller than a grain of rice. These are eaten by small fish, which are then eaten by larger fish, so the plastic moves up the food chain. Scientists have found microplastics in seafood sold for people to eat, although the full effect on human health is still being studied.
Plastic waste also damages coastlines. Beaches covered in rubbish drive away tourists, which hurts towns that depend on them, and cleaning up the mess costs governments large sums of money every year. It is, quite simply, a problem we have created, and one we will have to solve.

Summarise how plastic reaches the oceans and the harm it causes there.

BLOCK C
Walk through any busy Indian city in the evening, and you will smell it before you see it: the sizzle of frying samosas, the smoky scent of roasting corn, the sharp tang of tamarind. Street food is everywhere, and it is loved by almost everyone, from schoolchildren to office workers.
The first reason for its popularity is price. A plate of pav bhaji or a paper cone of roasted peanuts costs far less than a meal in a restaurant, so people on tight budgets can still eat well. For students like me, whose pocket money rarely lasts until the end of the month, this matters a great deal.
Street food is also quick. Most stalls cook and serve in minutes, which suits people rushing home from work or college. There is no waiting for a table and no menu to study; you simply point, pay and eat.
Another reason is variety. Every region has its own specialities, so a traveller can taste a different snack in every city, and even on every street. Many stalls have been run by the same family for generations, and some vendors guard their recipes as closely as a bank guards its gold.
Finally, street food is social. Friends gather around a chaat stall after school, families stop for kulfi on summer nights, and strangers chat while waiting for their order. For many people, eating on the street is not just about food; it is about being part of the life of the city.

Summarise the reasons why street food is so popular.
